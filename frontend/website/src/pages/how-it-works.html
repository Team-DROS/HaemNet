How it works

One request, many donors, one live view.

This is what happens from the moment a hospital submits an emergency request.

  1. The hospital submits a request

    A signed-in hospital chooses the blood group, number of units, urgency and location. Blood group can be a single group, All positive or All negative.

  2. HaemNet searches for donors

    It finds registered donors of the requested group within 10 km of the hospital who are outside the 56-day donation cooldown.

  3. AI voice calls go out at the same time

    Every matched donor receives a call in their preferred language: English, Hindi or Tamil. The agent explains who is calling, why, and asks if the donor can help.

  4. The donor confirms, separately

    If the donor sounds willing, the agent asks one more question to confirm. Only a clear yes at that second step is recorded as acceptance. A refusal is recorded as a decline.

  5. Directions arrive by SMS

    Once a donor accepts, HaemNet sends directions so they can reach the hospital.

  6. The hospital tracks and records

    The dashboard updates live as donors answer. When someone actually donates, hospital staff record it, which updates the donor's cooldown and the request's fulfilment.

Behind the call

What the AI does and does not do.

What it does

  • Understands the donor's spoken reply using Sarvam AI speech and language models.
  • Asks a separate confirmation question before recording a yes.
  • Speaks back in the donor's chosen language.
  • Reports every outcome to the hospital dashboard.

What it does not do

  • It does not give medical advice or judge medical eligibility.
  • It does not replace the hospital's screening and cross-matching.
  • It does not treat silence, a maybe or an error as a yes.
  • It does not track donors' live location.

HaemNet uses pretrained models. It does not train its own models on donor conversations. The 56-day cooldown is an application rule and not a complete medical eligibility check.
